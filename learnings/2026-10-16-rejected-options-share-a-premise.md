# When every option is rejected, the premise they share is the problem

**What happened.** Photos on a dark slide deck had a stepped, pixel-block edge. Asked for
alternatives, four edge treatments (a rounded window, a fade, a glowing line, a half-round
portal) all came back rejected. Each was a variation on the same premise: a gray photo,
framed, beside the title.

**Why.** Varying the parameter the user named (the edge) kept the thing they were reacting
to (a desaturated framed photo fighting a deck built of light). Options that share a premise
are one option.

**Do instead.** Before making variants, name the premise and offer options that differ in
it (here: the photo as a tinted backdrop, inside a large numeral, or gone). Tie them to the
work's theme, and say what the theme is.

**Evidence.** The second round offered three directions and one was picked; the first round
of four was rejected outright.
